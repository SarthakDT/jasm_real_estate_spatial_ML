# JASM Spatial ML — Project Plan & Work Log

---

## 1. Project Overview

**JASM Spatial ML** is a geospatial machine learning pipeline built around official Japanese land valuation data for Kumamoto Prefecture. It tracks how benchmark land prices change year-over-year across hundreds of survey plots, models those transitions using spatial and temporal features, and ultimately produces predictions of future land valuations.

The name encodes the core idea: **J**apanese **A**sset **S**patial **M**achine Learning.

---

## 2. The Problem Being Addressed

Land valuation in Japan is a dual-layer system. The government publishes annual *Chika-Kouji* (地価公示) benchmark prices at fixed survey plots, and financial institutions, tax authorities, and urban planners all rely on this data. Kumamoto Prefecture became a particularly interesting case after the 2016 Kumamoto Earthquake and subsequent reconstruction — land prices across the prefecture did not move uniformly. Some municipal zones rebounded sharply; others stagnated or declined.

The core research question this project addresses:

> **Can a model trained on a benchmark plot's location, its surrounding municipality, and its historical price trajectory predict the direction and magnitude of its next-year price change?**

This is valuable because:
- Manual appraisal cycles are expensive and slow.
- Spatial correlation between adjacent plots is currently underutilised.
- A model that learns municipality-level and coordinate-level pricing patterns can surface anomalies (under- or over-valued plots) faster than the traditional process.

## 4. Data Schema — Stage 1 Contract

The pipeline produces a **transition row** for each benchmark plot for each consecutive year pair starting from 2021. Each row captures one year-over-year price movement event.

| Column | Type | Description | Validation Rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| `point_id` | `object` | Unique identifier: `{muni_code}_{lat}_{lon}_Y{year}` | Zero duplicates allowed |
| `municipality` | `object` | Human-readable municipal name (e.g. `Kikuyo-cho`) | Must match 24 known Kumamoto names |
| `latitude` | `float64` | WGS84 north/south coordinate | Strictly between `32.0` and `33.2` |
| `longitude` | `float64` | WGS84 east/west coordinate | Strictly between `130.0` and `131.2` |
| `past_year_price` | `int64` | Land price per m² at year *t−1* (¥) | Must be > 0 |
| `current_year_price` | `int64` | Land price per m² at year *t* (¥) | Must be > 0 |
| `years_since_announcement` | `int64` | Years elapsed since 2021 (the anchor year) | Integer in range [0, 6] |

**Anchor year:** 2021 was chosen as the announcement year because it marks the start of the post-earthquake recovery monitoring window. `years_since_announcement = 0` means the transition is 2020 → 2021; `= 5` means 2025 → 2026.

### CRS Note — Tokyo Datum Trap

Legacy MLIT data occasionally ships in **Tokyo Datum (旧日本測地系)** instead of modern **WGS84**. The two systems differ by roughly **400–450 metres** near Kumamoto. The pipeline detects this by verifying that all coordinates fall within the known WGS84 bounding box for Kumamoto (`lat 32.0–33.2`, `lon 130.0–131.2`). Points outside this box trigger a hard validation error identifying suspected datum contamination.

### Stage 1 — Data Ingestion & Schema Validation

**Status:** Complete

**Objective:** Ingest all 8 years of raw MLIT GeoJSON, build year-over-year price transition rows, enforce the Stage 1 schema contract, and write `data/processed_stage1.csv`.

**Implementation:** `src/load_data.py` (320 lines)

**Key engineering decisions:**

- **Schema mapping dictionary** — Rather than a flat column rename (which would break on MLIT's 2022 and 2024 key changes), the pipeline uses a range-keyed dict that selects the correct property names per year at load time.
- **Transition builder** — Raw records are grouped by a stable `plot_key` (`muni_code + rounded lat/lon`) and sorted by year. Only consecutive year pairs (`curr_year − prev_year == 1`) starting at or after 2021 are emitted as rows, ensuring `years_since_announcement >= 0` always holds.
- **Coordinate rounding** — Coordinates are rounded to 5 decimal places (~1.1 m precision) before building the plot key. This absorbs minor GPS measurement variance between annual survey editions while keeping the same physical plot matched across years.
- **Null strategy** — Rows missing either price value are dropped (appraisal cycles occasionally skip rural plots). Auxiliary NaN values (lat/lon/YSA) are imputed with the per-municipality median, with a global median as fallback.
- **Encoding fix** — GeoJSON input switched from `utf-8` to `utf-8-sig` to safely strip the UTF-8 BOM present in some older MLIT releases without affecting BOM-free files.

### Stage 2 — Geospatial Feature Engineering

**Status:** Complete

**Objective:** Transform raw lat/lon coordinates into three high-signal numeric columns that encode the economic geography of the JASM fab site, producing `data/processed_stage2.csv` as input to Stage 3.

**Implementation:** `src/features.py` (128 lines)

**Key engineering decisions:**

- **Geodesic distance via WGS84 ellipsoid** — Used `geopy.distance.geodesic` rather than planar Pythagorean distance. The Haversine approximation introduces sub-metre error at the scale of Kumamoto; geodesic is the correct standard for ellipsoidal Earth geometry.
- **Inverse spatial decay with zero-division guard** — `1 / (distance_km + 1)` bounds the feature to `(0, 1]`. The `+1` offset means a hypothetical plot exactly at the JASM gates yields `1.0` without a `ZeroDivisionError`, while plots at 106 km yield `≈ 0.009`.
- **Target as percentage change** — `((current − past) / past) × 100` expresses the label in directly interpretable economic units (percentage points) rather than raw yen deltas, which would be dominated by high-value commercial outliers.
- **Input guard** — Raises `FileNotFoundError` / `ValueError` with instructive messages if `processed_stage1.csv` is absent or missing expected columns, preventing silent failures in CI.

### Stage 3 — Model Architecture & Training

**Status:** Complete

**Objective:** Train a Random Forest Regressor on the six numeric features from Stage 2, evaluate it on a held-out 25% test partition, serialize the trained model to `models/jasm_rf_model.pkl`, and produce the DoD health report.

**Implementation:** `src/train.py` (130 lines)

**Key engineering decisions:**

- **Random Forest over linear regression** — Land price response to fab proximity is non-linear (high pressure within 5 km, diminishing returns beyond 20 km). Linear models cannot capture these regional thresholds without manual polynomial expansion.
- **Strict feature exclusion** — `municipality` (categorical string), `point_id` (index), and `current_year_price` (direct component of the target) are excluded from X to prevent target leakage. Only the six continuous/discrete numeric features enter the model.
- **Hyperparameter constraints against overfitting** — `max_depth=7` and `min_samples_split=5` prevent the trees from memorising individual coordinate identities. The 0.22 gap between train R2 (0.85) and test R2 (0.63) is expected and healthy given the dataset size.
- **Deterministic seed** — `random_state=42` on both the split and the model ensures the exact same 1,064/355 partition and forest structure are reproduced on every run.
